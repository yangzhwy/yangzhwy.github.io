I’m a doctoral fellow at [MCMP](https://www.philosophie.lmu.de/mcmp/en/) at LMU München.

My primary research interests are in epistemology, especially the epistemology of understanding. In my PhD, I focus on mathematical understanding and argue for a pluralist view of understanding by analyzing mathematical practices in terms of intellectual virtue.

More broadly, I’m interested in the different forms that understanding can take and in how they are achieved through different kinds of goal-oriented performances. I think at least some very different forms of understanding can be explained in sufficiently similar ways by looking at how we exercise our cognitive competences. Ultimately, I hope to contribute to building a bridge between mathematical understanding and more ordinary kinds of understanding. 

That is also why I started with mathematical understanding. Among the different kinds of understanding, I always find understanding of mathematical objects achieved through mathematical practices one of the easiest to characterize due to the exactness of modern mathematics. Modern mathematics is exact in making explicit what a mathematician is working on, what she tries to achieve and what counts as success. But the funny thing is, when I tell people about the topic of my dissertation, people commonly find it impressive. I guess that is because they first think of how hard mathematics is. Luckily for me, my work is more philosophical than mathematical. I’m interested much more in whether we can learn more from mathematical understanding to help us make sense of the much messier kinds of understanding we achieve in ordinary life.

One of my intellectual role model is Susanne Langer. What I find inspiring about her is the way she began from a philosophical background closely connected with logic–the kind of analytic philosophy associated with Carnap and the Vienna Circle–but then pushed those philosophical interests into much broader domains including music, art, and aesthetics. For me, her trajectory suggests a way of starting with forms of understanding that can be approached through relatively precise logical methods and then moving toward other forms of understanding for which we may need new philosophical methods and new conceptual tools. That is very close to the direction in which I hope my own work can develop.

My epistemological interests is not restricted to human understanding but concern also the understanding achieved by other kinds of intellectual agents such as other animals, aliens or AIs, as long as they can exercise cognitive competences that are comparable to ours. I find it cool that this notion of understanding is not a recent one. Even philosophers such as Kant were already interested thinking about different possible forms of cognition in terms of different applications of cognitive faculties. What is even cooler is that this connects surprisingly well with the direction of my own research: thank to Hannes’s suggestion, I follow Sosa in thinking about epistemic success in terms of intellectual virtue, while Aristotles gives us a much earlier example of thinking about different forms of successful performances through the relevant virtue.

Beside my academic life, I’m a cat-person, a scuba-diver, a trilingual speaker, and a mind-wanderer. I try to follow the advice of Kate, one of my role models in real life: stop questioning, just do it!!
